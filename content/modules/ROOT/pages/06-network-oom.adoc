= Module 4: Compare an OOM remediation
:toc: macro
:toclevels: 1

The next alert describes memory exhaustion. Compare the selected action with the CrashLoop incident and assess whether the automation choice matches the evidence.

Estimated time: 10 minutes.

== Learning objectives

* Compare two incidents without mixing their IDs or artifacts.
* Explain why a resource-exhaustion assessment can select a different template.
* Recognize independent edge actuation and duplicate-action suppression.

== Exercise 1: Run the OOM scenario

. In the *Network dashboard*, click *Trigger OOM Demo* once under *Demo Mode*.
. Record the new *Incident ID*, *Topic*, *Offset*, *Target*, and *Event*. Keep these separate from the CrashLoop notes. The simulated message describes an `OOMKilled` condition, an exceeded memory limit, and restart history.
. Expand *Incident Timeline* and locate the card with this incident ID after processing completes. Record the stage, summary, *AAP Job*, and *SNOW* fields.
. In the application namespace's `agent-service` pod *Logs*, correlate the topic and offset with the workflow completion containing your incident ID, as in the previous module. Inspect available investigation, retrieval, analysis, and guard entries.
. Compare the assessed failure type and action with the CrashLoop run. The expected template for an `OOMKilled` assessment is `scale-up-workers`, subject to sufficient confidence and the playbook-match guard. Record the action that actually ran, or the reason it did not run, wherever the logs or summary expose it.

=== Verify

* The OOM and CrashLoop incidents have separate correlation notes.
* Your OOM result is based on its own matching card and available logs.
* You have recorded the selected action and mock job outcome, or the actual escalation or duplicate-action suppression outcome.

. Explain why restarting a stalled worker and addressing resource exhaustion are different responses. The structured assessment guides the template mapping, and retrieved runbooks provide context. Neither a scenario name nor an LLM confidence score proves that a proposed change fixes the real workload.
. Check whether the summary mentions that the deployment was already remediated and a duplicate AAP job was skipped. The independent edge fast-path watcher and runner can patch `edge-nginx`. A correlated recent edge heal can suppress the OOM flow's duplicate hub automation, leaving no AAP job ID for that action.
. If your result shows a successful mock job, describe precisely what succeeded: the mock accepted the automation request and returned its simulated job result. Record real workload observations separately. Do not attribute a change made by the edge runner to the AAP mock.

[NOTE]
====
`scale-up-workers` is the demo's mapped template for resource exhaustion. This exercise evaluates selection and job handling. A real deployment requires a playbook and parameters appropriate to the actual workload, plus recovery checks.
====

=== Verify

* Your comparison explains the difference between the proposed restart and resource-exhaustion actions.
* An empty AAP job field is interpreted alongside the stage, summary, and logs.
* Your conclusion distinguishes real observations and edge mutations from simulated alert input and mock execution.

=== Troubleshooting

An escalation can be valid when the analysis or guard does not support automation. A missing job is also consistent with an explicitly recorded duplicate-action skip. If neither is recorded and the incident remains absent, revisit xref:04-readiness.adoc[deployment readiness]. Do not use *Reset Lightspeed Demo* to reset an OOM run; it targets a different demo outside this walkthrough.

== Check your understanding

Does a different template prove that the system diagnosed memory exhaustion correctly?

Expected answer: no. It shows a different automation choice. Assess the available Kubernetes evidence, retrieved guidance, analysis, and guard outcome, then verify recovery independently when using real automation.
