= Module 6: Explore RAN scenarios
:toc: macro
:toclevels: 1

Try a congestion scenario, then send normal traffic to see when the RAN workflow takes action.

*Estimated time:* 10 minutes.

== Learning objectives

* Follow a congestion diagnosis to its mapped automation job.
* Compare the visible results of fault and normal-traffic samples.

== Exercise 1: Try a congestion scenario

This sample represents gradually increasing network congestion. The intended classification maps to the `ran-load-balance` AAP template, which updates the RAN simulator. Check the actual classification and job outcome on the incident card.

. In the RAN dashboard, select *Anomaly List*. Under *Demo Mode*, click *High Network Congestion (Gradual Buildup)* once.
+
.Example congestion trigger.
image::ran-congestion-trigger.png[Congestion demo trigger showing incident ID topic and offset,link=self,window=blank]
+
. Wait for the new card under *Recent Anomalies*. Use the incident ID returned by the trigger to identify it.
. Read *Mantis RCA*, *LLM Explanation*, and *Recommended Fix*. The intended classification is `High Network Congestion (Gradual Buildup)`, with a recommendation addressing congestion. Model predictions and explanation wording can vary.
+
.Example congestion incident from a run with mocked AAP. The job output is simulated; your lab uses real AAP.
image::ran-congestion-incident.png[Congestion incident card showing classification explanation and simulated job outcome,link=self,window=blank]
+
. Check *Job* and *Job outcome* on the same card. The congestion category maps to `ran-load-balance`; a successful job shows the *Remediated* badge.

=== Verify

* The new incident card shows a diagnosis and recommended fix.
* The card shows the mapped automation job and its outcome.

== Exercise 2: Observe normal traffic

Normal traffic is expected to stop at anomaly detection. The detector does not expose a per-sample completion result for normal predictions, so this exercise records an observation rather than confirming normal classification.

. Wait for the congestion scenario to finish and check that the dashboard shows no required dependency failures.
. Click *Normal Traffic (No Anomaly)* once. Record the returned incident ID, topic, offset, and publication time.
. Observe *Recent Anomalies* for one minute. Look for the returned incident ID; earlier fault cards remain visible. Check recent AAP jobs for that ID in job details or variables.
. Record one result: no matching card or job observed, a matching anomaly appeared, or dependency errors prevented the check. Stop the observation after one minute; this interval is an exercise limit, not a processing guarantee.

=== Verify

* You recorded the sample's identifiers, publication time, and observation result.
* You distinguish successful publication and an absence of visible action from confirmed normal processing.

If dependencies fail or processing is in doubt, open the `ran-anomaly-detector` pod's *Logs* in `{app_namespace}`. Find `ran-metrics message received` with the input topic and offset and check for handler or predictor errors. This confirms receipt, not a normal prediction. Record unresolved processing as unconfirmed and share the identifiers with the facilitator.

== Troubleshooting

Do not ask RAN Chat to confirm a normal incident by ID: chat uses buffered anomalies and can fall back to the latest anomaly when an ID is absent. Always verify the ID in any answer. The *Clear* button clears dashboard service buffers; it does not reset the pipeline, remove Kafka history, or prove normal detection.
